# Insurance & Surety Bonds — Market Context

## Market Size and Growth

- **Total Brazilian insurance market**: R$751.3B in 2024 (12.2% growth)
- **Seguro garantia segment**: R$6.27B in 2025, growing 23.2% — the fastest-growing segment in Brazilian insurance
- The seguro garantia market has **doubled since 2020** and is projected to surpass **R$10B by 2028**
- **44 active insurers** in the segment, up 22% in five years
- **86.9% of seguro garantia operations** are tied to public sector contracts

## Industry Structure

The seguro garantia value chain involves three key parties: the **tomador** (contractor/obligor who needs the bond), the **segurado** (beneficiary, typically a government entity), and the **seguradora** (insurer issuing the bond). Corretoras (brokers) play a critical intermediary role, with specialized firms like Granto operating as digital-first distribution platforms.

The segment is structurally driven by **Lei 14.133/2021 (Nova Lei de Licitações)**, which expanded seguro garantia requirements:
- Article 96: guarantees of up to **5% of contract value** (10% with justification)
- Article 101: guarantees of up to **30% of contract value** with a **step-in clause** enabling the insurer to take over and complete projects — a game-changer for infrastructure

## Technology Adoption Landscape

A CNseg/EY study from February 2026 reveals the state of AI adoption in Brazilian insurance:
- **80% of Brazilian insurers have adopted AI** in some form
- Only **23% see meaningful impact** from their AI investments
- The main barrier is **legacy system integration (69%)** — not AI technology itself

This gap between AI adoption and impact represents JP Ventures' core opportunity: specialized integration of AI into underwriting, claims, and compliance workflows is where the ROI lives.

## Data Maturity Gap

Mid-market seguro garantia insurers typically suffer from:
- **Disconnected core systems**: policy admin, CRM, claims, and financial systems that don't share data
- **Manual underwriting workflows**: credit analysis involves manual lookups across multiple sources (credit bureaus, court records, SUSEP data)
- **Spreadsheet-based compliance**: SUSEP reporting involves manual data extraction and formatting
- **Limited API capabilities**: many legacy policy admin systems lack modern API interfaces
- **No real-time data access**: batch-oriented processes that can't support real-time decision-making

## Macro Trends

1. **Lei 14.133/2021 expansion**: The Nova Lei de Licitações expanded seguro garantia requirements, structurally growing demand. The 30% step-in clause creates entirely new product categories.

2. **Rising sinistralidade**: Loss ratio rose **16 percentage points to 41.7% in 2025**, making AI-driven claims intelligence and underwriting optimization urgent for profitability.

3. **Lei 15.040/2024 (Marco Legal dos Seguros)**: Entered into force December 2025, creating a new insurance contract framework requiring **25-day proposal response**, **30-day claims response**, and enhanced transparency.

4. **Open Insurance (OPIN)**: Phase 3 (service initiation) went live in 2025, making Brazil a **global pioneer in open insurance implementation**. First-movers gain new acquisition channels.

5. **Digital-first competition**: Players like Granto and Brick are setting new standards for speed and automation, forcing traditional insurers to modernize or lose market share.
