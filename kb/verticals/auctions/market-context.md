# Auction Market — Market Context

## Market Size and Growth

Brazil's auction market is experiencing extraordinary, structural growth:

- **86% increase in auction volume in 2024**, with 275,000 total transactions moving **R$200 billion**
- Caixa Economica Federal alone auctioned **47,000 properties in 2024** (up from 9,000 in 2022 — a 5x increase), with stock reaching 50,400 units
- **H1 2025**: 116,600 properties auctioned, up **25.1% year-over-year**
- Google searches for "leilao de imoveis" grew **over 3,000%** in the past 12 months
- Caixa spent **R$443M in 2023 on IPTU and condominio alone** for its unsold property stock, creating strong institutional motivation to accelerate sell-through

## Industry Structure

The auction market operates through a fragmented ecosystem:

- **Leiloeiros**: Over 1,000 individual leiloeiro websites in Brazil, each with different formats, no standardized data, and no centralized database
- **Banks**: Caixa Economica Federal (dominant seller of alienacao fiduciaria properties), Banco do Brasil, Santander — each with proprietary platforms
- **TJ courts**: 27 different state Tribunal de Justica systems managing judicial auctions
- **Aggregators**: Platforms consolidating multiple leiloeiro sources (e.g., Leilao Imovel with 800+ leiloeiros)
- **White-label providers**: SaaS platforms serving leiloeiros (e.g., Sua Plataforma de Leilao serving 100+ leiloeiros)
- **Investor groups and advisory firms**: Professional investors and clubs screening deals

## Hidden Cost Structure (The 40% Reserve Rule)

Investors must reserve **40%+ above the lance (bid) value** for hidden costs:

| Cost Category | Typical Percentage |
|---------------|-------------------|
| ITBI (property transfer tax) | 2-3% of arrematacao value (varies by municipality) |
| Leiloeiro commission | 5% (per CNJ Resolucao 236) |
| Cartorio fees | 1-2% |
| Reforma (renovation) | Variable |
| Legal fees | 3-5% |
| Hidden debts (IPTU, condominio) | Variable — Caixa spent R$443M in 2023 on IPTU/condominio alone |

## Technology Adoption Landscape

Technology adoption in Brazil's auction market is nascent. AI-powered platforms are emerging but the market remains overwhelmingly manual:

- **Manual edital analysis** costs ~R$2,000 per property for lawyer review
- **Matricula checks** for encumbrances require manual cartorio visits
- **Property valuation** demands cross-referencing FipeZap, OLX, ZAP, and QuintoAndar data by hand
- **Deal screening** is limited to whatever fraction of 1,000+ sites an investor can manually monitor

Emerging AI tools are beginning to address specific pain points (see [competitive-intel.md](competitive-intel.md) for details).

## Data Maturity Gap

The auction vertical has one of the most severe data fragmentation problems of any Brazilian market:

- **1,000+ leiloeiro websites** with heterogeneous formats
- **No unified property identifier** across systems (matricula numbers are cartorio-specific)
- **No centralized database** of auction properties
- **PDF editais** require OCR for data extraction
- **27 different TJ systems** for judicial auction data
- **JavaScript-rendered sites** with CAPTCHAs and session-based authentication frustrate aggregation
- **Property addresses** need geocoding for map visualization

## Macro Trends

1. **Institutional sell-through pressure**: Banks holding large property portfolios (Caixa's 50,400 units) are motivated to improve sell-through from the current 15% rate
2. **Democratization of access**: The 3,000% Google search increase signals massive new buyer interest from individuals
3. **Regulatory evolution**: Lei 14.711/2023 updates alienacao fiduciaria procedures, creating integration requirements
4. **Professionalization of investor base**: Growing demand for institutional-grade analytics at individual investor prices
5. **AI-native competition**: Emergence of Leilao Ninja, BuscAI, Arremata.ai signals market readiness for technology solutions
