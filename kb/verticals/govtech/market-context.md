# GovTech — Market Context

## Market Size and Growth

- **Annual public procurement (Brazil)**: R$800B+ across federal, state, and municipal levels
- **Procurement entities**: 200,000+ (federal agencies, state secretarias, municipal prefeituras, autarquias, fundacoes, empresas publicas)
- **Municipalities**: 5,570, each with independent procurement processes
- **PNCP registered contracts**: Millions and growing daily as Lei 14.133/2021 adoption expands
- **GovTech ecosystem**: 100+ startups in the BrazilLAB ecosystem; estimated R$2B+ addressable market for GovTech solutions
- **Tribunais de Contas**: 33 (1 TCU + 26 TCEs + 4 TCMs + TCE-RJ + TCE-SP covering municipalities)
- **Federal transparency budget**: Portal da Transparencia tracks R$3T+ in federal expenditure

## Industry Structure

The Brazilian public procurement and transparency ecosystem operates across three tiers with distinct pain points:

**Federal Level**: TCU (audit), CGU (internal control), Ministerio da Gestao e Inovacao (procurement policy/PNCP). ComprasNet transitioning to PNCP. SIAFI for financial execution, SICONV/+Brasil for transfers (convenios). Well-funded but bureaucratic adoption cycles.

**State Level**: 26 TCEs + 4 TCMs perform external audit. State Controladorias handle internal control. Each state has its own procurement portal (BEC-SP, CELIC-RS, SIAD-MG). High variability in digital maturity -- Sao Paulo and Minas Gerais are advanced; northern states lag significantly.

**Municipal Level**: 5,570 municipalities, most with minimal IT infrastructure. Rely on third-party procurement systems (Betha, Fly, IPM). Diarios Oficiais as primary publication channel. Massive fragmentation creates opportunity for aggregation platforms.

**Private Sector (Demand Side)**: Companies selling to government need procurement intelligence -- monitoring opportunities, qualifying bids, tracking contracts. Insurance brokers (seguro garantia), construction companies, IT service providers, and medical suppliers are the largest segments.

## Technology Adoption Landscape

The GovTech technology landscape is in rapid transition:

- **ComprasNet to PNCP migration**: The 25-year-old ComprasNet system is being replaced by PNCP (Lei 14.133/2021). PNCP offers API access, standardized data formats, and centralized publication. Transition is ongoing with dual operation.
- **SEI (Sistema Eletronico de Informacoes)**: Adopted by 150+ federal agencies and expanding to states/municipalities. Digital process management replacing paper workflows.
- **+Brasil (formerly SICONV)**: Platform for federal transfers (convenios, contratos de repasse). R$100B+ in tracked transfers.
- **TCU Alice/Adele**: AI systems for automated edital analysis and audit. Alice analyzes editais for irregularities; Adele assists auditors. Proof of concept for AI in government audit.
- **Querido Diario (OKBR)**: Open-source project parsing Diarios Oficiais from 4,000+ municipalities into structured, searchable data. Critical infrastructure for procurement intelligence.
- **e-SIC / Fala.BR**: Citizen information request platforms under LAI (Lei 12.527/2011). Data source for transparency analytics.

## Data Maturity

Public procurement data in Brazil is rich but severely fragmented:

| Dimension | Current State | Impact |
|-----------|--------------|--------|
| Data Centralization | PNCP consolidating but still incomplete; many entities publish only locally | No single source of truth for all procurement |
| Data Standardization | Lei 14.133/2021 defines standards but adoption varies | Inconsistent data formats across entities |
| API Access | PNCP offers APIs; most state/municipal portals do not | Scraping required for comprehensive coverage |
| Historical Data | ComprasNet archives exist but migration to PNCP is incomplete | Historical analysis requires multi-source integration |
| Supplier Data | CEIS/CNEP/CEPIM track sanctions; SICAF tracks qualifications | Fragmented supplier intelligence across systems |
| Transparency | Portal da Transparencia excellent at federal level; varies wildly at municipal level | Uneven coverage creates blind spots |

## Macro Trends

1. **PNCP centralization**: First time Brazil has a single, API-accessible procurement database. Creates the data foundation for analytics that was previously impossible.
2. **Estrategia de Governo Digital 2024--2027 (EGD)**: Federal strategy mandating digital services, interoperability, and AI adoption across government. Decreto 11.260/2022 establishes governance.
3. **AI for audit**: TCU's Alice and Adele systems have demonstrated ROI. State TCEs are seeking similar capabilities but lack in-house AI expertise.
4. **Open Data movement**: Lei 12.527/2011 (LAI) + Decreto 8.777/2016 (Dados Abertos) + OKBR's Querido Diario creating unprecedented access to government data.
5. **Anti-corruption enforcement**: Operacao Lava Jato legacy + Lei 12.846/2013 (Anti-Corruption Law) + Lei 8.429/1992 (Improbidade) driving demand for compliance and due diligence tools.
6. **Municipal digital transformation**: Smaller municipalities adopting digital procurement systems, creating demand for aggregation and intelligence layers.
