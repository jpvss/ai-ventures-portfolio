# Construction — Market Context

## Market Size and Growth

- **PIB Construcao**: ~R$500B annually, representing 6.2% of Brazilian GDP
- **Active construction companies**: 250,000+ registered with CNAE codes in the construction sector
- **Formal employment**: ~2.5M workers in formal construction jobs (RAIS/CAGED), with an estimated equal number informal
- **Public works share**: Federal, state, and municipal governments account for ~30% of total construction output, with Lei 14.133/2021 governing all public procurement
- **PAC (Programa de Aceleracao do Crescimento)**: Multi-year infrastructure program injecting R$1.7T+ over 4 years, with significant construction component
- **Residential construction**: Minha Casa Minha Vida (MCMV) program alone represents R$100B+ in annual Caixa-funded residential construction, all requiring SINAPI compliance

## Industry Structure

The Brazilian construction value chain is highly fragmented. At the top, large construtoras (MRV, Cyrela, Direcional, Tenda) execute mega-projects with sophisticated ERP systems. The mid-market (R$10M-R$200M revenue) — our primary target — comprises thousands of regional construtoras that handle public works, commercial construction, and residential projects using basic tools: spreadsheets, disconnected ERPs, and manual processes.

Key segments within our target market:

| Segment | Size Estimate | Key Characteristics |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Construtoras de medio porte** | 5,000-10,000 firms (R$10M-R$200M) | Regional, multi-project, mix of public/private works |
| **Construtoras de obras publicas** | 3,000-5,000 firms | Licitacao-dependent, SINAPI-compliant, bond requirements |
| **Administradoras de condominios** | 10,000+ firms | High volume, low margin, back-office intensive |
| **Incorporadoras (mid-market)** | 2,000-3,000 firms | SPE-based, financial management intensive (see incorporacoes-gestao) |

### Large Players (Reference, Not Primary Targets)

Large construtoras serve as reference benchmarks for technology adoption. MRV has invested heavily in construction tech (Luggo platform), Cyrela uses advanced BIM workflows, and Andrade Gutierrez/Odebrecht (Novonor) deploy enterprise ERP/project management. These firms set the standard that mid-market must eventually match.

## Technology Adoption Landscape

Brazilian mid-market construction has one of the lowest technology adoption rates of any major sector:

- **ERP adoption**: ~40% of mid-market construtoras use a dedicated construction ERP (primarily Sienge); the remainder use generic ERPs (TOTVS), spreadsheets, or nothing
- **BIM adoption**: Less than 20% of mid-market firms use BIM regularly, despite Decreto 10.306/2020 mandating BIM for federal public works
- **Cost control**: 70%+ of mid-market firms rely on spreadsheets for cost tracking, with data reconciliation happening monthly or quarterly — not in real time
- **Licitacao monitoring**: Most firms rely on manual searches of Diarios Oficiais and word-of-mouth, covering perhaps 5-10% of available opportunities

This technology gap is AI Ventures' core opportunity: the data infrastructure required to digitize construction operations creates a natural platform for AI-powered cost intelligence, procurement monitoring, and compliance automation.

## Data Maturity Gap

Mid-market construtoras typically suffer from:

- **Disconnected project data**: Cost data, schedule data, safety data, and financial data exist in separate spreadsheets/systems per project with no consolidation
- **Manual cost benchmarking**: SINAPI compositions (100K+ items) are looked up manually for budget preparation and cost validation
- **Delayed variance detection**: Budget vs. actual comparison happens monthly at best, meaning overruns of 10-20% are discovered 2-3 months late
- **No licitacao intelligence**: Monitoring public procurement across 5,570 municipalities is impossible manually; firms cover only their immediate region
- **Paper-based compliance**: Safety inspections (NR-18), environmental permits, and construction licenses tracked on paper or basic spreadsheets
- **Limited historical analytics**: Past project data is archived in disconnected files, making it impossible to learn from historical cost patterns or schedule performance

## Macro Trends

1. **PAC infrastructure boom (2024-2027)**: R$1.7T+ in infrastructure investment creates massive pipeline of public works requiring SINAPI/SICRO compliance, generating demand for cost intelligence and licitacao monitoring tools.

2. **BIM mandate escalation (Decreto 10.306/2020)**: Federal public works increasingly require BIM adoption. Mid-market firms that cannot deliver BIM-compliant projects will be excluded from the largest, most profitable contracts.

3. **Lei 14.133/2021 digital-first procurement**: The new procurement law mandates electronic licitacoes and PNCP publication, creating structured data feeds that can be monitored programmatically — a game-changer for firms with data infrastructure.

4. **Construction cost volatility**: Post-pandemic material price swings (steel, cement, aggregates) and labor cost increases make real-time cost monitoring and predictive analytics essential for margin protection.

5. **Industrialized construction growth**: Pre-fabrication, modular construction, and 3D printing are gaining traction, requiring more sophisticated planning and data integration than traditional methods.

6. **ESG and sustainability requirements**: Green building certifications (LEED, AQUA), carbon accounting, and environmental compliance create new data streams that must be integrated into project management.
