## Win/Loss Patterns

### Common Win Factors

- Dealer has experienced inventory losses (vehicles sold below acquisition cost) and can quantify the pain
- Owner/Director personally involved — not delegated to an IT person with no budget authority
- Current pricing is gut-based or FIPE-only — clear room for improvement
- Floor plan interest is a visible cost line item (high SELIC makes this acute)
- Dealer is growing (opening new locations, increasing stock) and realizes current process doesn't scale
- Has seen digital-first competitors (InstaCarro, Volanty) taking market share

### Common Loss Factors

- Very small operation (<30 vehicles) — economics don't justify investment
- Owner believes pricing is an art, not a science — resistant to data-driven approach
- Currently in financial distress — no budget for investment even if ROI is clear
- Expects a magic button that eliminates all human judgment — mismatched expectations
- Already contracted with a general-purpose consulting firm for broader digital transformation

### Objection Patterns

| Objection | Response | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| "I've been doing this for 20 years, I know my market" | "Your experience is invaluable — we encode it into a model that works 24/7 across every vehicle. The model doesn't replace your judgment; it gives you data to sharpen it." | Pricing accuracy comparison: gut vs. model-assisted |
| "FIPE is good enough" | "FIPE is a monthly average. The same car is worth 15% more in Ribeirão Preto than in Recife. A 30K km car vs. 120K km — same FIPE. Your margin lives in these gaps." | Regional price variation analysis; condition-based price spread |
| "Too expensive for a used car dealer" | "You're paying R$500–R$2,000 per month for every car that sits too long. With 200 cars averaging 10 extra days, that's R$100K–R$400K/year in carrying costs alone. Our R$100K project pays for itself in 3–6 months." | Floor plan cost calculation |
| "I can build a spreadsheet for this" | "A spreadsheet can't scrape Webmotors in real time, can't assess condition from photos, can't predict demand shifts. And it won't alert you at 3am when a competitor undercuts your pricing on 15 vehicles." | Feature comparison: spreadsheet vs. AI engine |

## Market Signals

- **Kavak exit (2023)**: Validated market need, proved vertical integration fails; creates opening for asset-light technology solutions
- **Rising SELIC (~13.25%)**: Floor plan costs make inventory intelligence existential, not optional
- **Digital marketplace saturation**: Consumers compare prices on OLX/Webmotors before visiting dealers — pricing transparency is forced
- **Vehicle-backed fintech growth**: New entrants (Creditas, BV, Carro Capital) need better collateral valuation than FIPE
- **EV uncertainty**: Electric vehicle residual values are unpredictable; creates demand for ML-based valuation models
- **Consolidation wave**: Larger dealer groups acquiring smaller operations need standardized data infrastructure
